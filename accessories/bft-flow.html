Accessory Board Test Flow

PDB · HPB · Bridge Board · VBB  |  as of 18 Aug 2026 Module flow →

The four boards that go into a 4U tray and a 2U system alongside the Sohu module, and what tests each one sees before it gets there. Board Function Test is the column this page exists for: it is the only stage that powers an accessory board up and talks to it, and today exactly one of the four boards has one running. The other three are proposed — drawn, costed, ticketed, and about twelve weeks from existing.

L6 — board level

Each board reads left to right through its own PCBA line and hands off to the assembly that consumes it. The stages are Pegatron's own, transcribed from the four PCBA Process Flow slides and the Aug 3 Production Test Flowchart. SMT here stands for the whole print-place-reflow-inspect chain on that slide (SPI, AOI top and bottom, 5DX X-ray, touch-up, de-panel).

Downstream — where the boards end up

Once a board leaves L6 it is never tested alone again: the next verdict it gets is a system one, with three other boards and a module in the same chassis. That is the argument for BFT in one sentence — a PDB fault found at 2U SFT costs a strip-down, and a PDB fault found at PDB BFT costs a board.

What each BFT would test

Test items exactly as the Pega proposal states them. All three share one rig: a Pega-owned test fixture motherboard with an AST2600 BMC, a Carol BMC card, a front-IO board, a host PC and a barcode reader — the board-specific part is the fixture board and its cables.

PDB BFT proposed

Everything the PDB does is pass power and the handshake around it, so every item is a pin test: drive one end from fixture GPIO, look for it at the other. No real power is applied — even the 54 V and 12 V nets are checked as continuity, not as rails.

  • Address / Preset / AC_FAULT pins
    Read the address pin level to confirm the resistor is mounted. TFMB sees 3.3 V or 0 V.
  • SMBUS DAT / INT# / CLK
    Drive high via PU1 to PSU1-4; TFMB checks the GPIO reaches 3.3 V.
  • PWROK
    Drive high via PSU1-4 to PU1; TFMB reads the PU1 PWROK pin.
  • PS_ON / 12VSB / SMART_ON / I_SHARE / RS+ / RS−
    Drive via PU1 to PSU1-4; TFMB reads the PSU PS_ON pin.
  • 54 V / 12 V pins
    Drive at PSU1, read at PSU1-4 and HPB_PSU2 — pin open or not, not a rail measurement.
Open since 30 Dec 2025 (Yibing): can we test the connection of each individual power or GND pin? Two answers are on the table and neither is chosen — Option 1 GPIO loopback through the DUT, Option 2 nine I2C IO expanders, send on one and listen on the other eight.

HPB BFT proposed

The only one of the three that actually powers the board: a golden PDB and a Pacer PSU feed real 54 V in, and the fixture drives the MP5920 hot-swap controllers and reads their registers back over I2C.

  • 54 V output
    TFMB drives the hot-swap EN; PG must come back at 3.3 V.
  • LED visual check
    Lit is a pass — the one item on any of the three that needs an operator's eye.
  • I2C
    Read the MPS register (e.g. 0x8B) and check the rail level is in range.
  • Reset
    Reset the HPB mux, then re-address it — the bus must come back.
  • 12 V output
    DUT supplies 12 V to the fixture; TFMB reads it high.
Open since 30 Dec 2025 (Yibing): can we add an external load — say 10 A — and measure the voltage drop from 54 V in to 54 V out? As written the test proves the rail comes up, not that it holds up.

BB BFT proposed

The bridge board is passive — eight connectors and the differential pairs between them — so there is one test item, and the board's whole quality argument rests on it. It is also the only board of the four with no ICT, which makes BFT its first and last electrical check.

  • Open / short
    All P signals of a connector pair are strapped together in the fixture, likewise all N. TFMB then proves P–N, P–GND and N–GND are all open.
Coverage gap, raised 30 Dec 2025 (Yibing): as designed this finds shorts — P to N, or either to GND — and cannot see an open circuit, which on a board that is nothing but interconnect is the failure that matters. The proposal on the following slide fixes it: cascade the P signals 1A→1B→1B→1A, toggle in from GPIO, monitor out, and require OUT to follow IN. Not yet in the test-item table.

Three things the flow makes obvious

BB has no electrical test at all

Every other board gets ICT. The bridge board's Pega slide draws X-ray and visual inspection and then hands it straight to 4U assembly. Until BB BFT exists, the first thing that can find a bad solder joint on it is a 4U tray test with a module and three other boards attached — and the proposed BFT, as written, still cannot see an open.

Twelve weeks is the whole schedule

All three carry the same ~12-week fixture lead time, and only two of the three drawings are released. Nothing about that clock starts until someone places the order, so today's decision is not "which test items" — it is whether to release the two finished drawings while the HPB one is still being drawn.

One station's worth of evidence

VBB BFT took a fortnight of on-site bring-up to get five of six items passing, still has an unstable PRBS, and runs over an hour a board on two fixtures whose connectors are rated for 300 insertions. Whatever the three new stations cost, that is the shape of it — and ETCH-20787, finding an owner for these three fixture designs, is still To Do.

No yields on this page, by design. Every stage on the module flow chart reports to a pega controller or to OCP Logs, so that page prints pass rates. None of the four accessory boards reports anywhere this repo can reach — VBB BFT results live on the station and its log access is still an open ticket (ETCH-39587). A green box here means the stage exists and runs, nothing more.
